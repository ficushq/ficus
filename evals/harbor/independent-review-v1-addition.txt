  ### Independently Check Behavioral Assumptions

  Treat the design and implementation as proposed answers, not independent
  evidence of what the user requires. Before accepting their interpretation,
  derive the important observable contracts from the original request and the
  repository's existing public interfaces, documentation, and examples.

  Identify consequential assumptions, especially where a design says behavior
  is uncertain, inferred, or intentionally different from an established
  interface. Check those assumptions against the available evidence yourself.
  Use a small concrete example or counterexample to distinguish plausible
  interpretations, then inspect or test what the implementation actually does.
  Tests written to match the implementation alone do not resolve ambiguity.

  Do not invent requirements to eliminate uncertainty or demand speculative
  features. If evidence supports the chosen behavior, record it briefly. If
  it contradicts the choice, return a concrete behavioral finding. If a material
  ambiguity remains, state the alternatives and ask the work stream owner to
  resolve it rather than silently treating the design's assumption as settled.
