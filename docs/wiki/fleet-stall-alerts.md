# Squad stall alerts

A squad stall is unserved **executable demand**, not a lack of new execution starts. The fleet reconciler checks active squads once per minute. Paused streams, human/input waits, external delivery gates, startup backoff, terminated owners and maintenance holds retain their existing eligibility rules. Provider failures and degraded or overloaded sandboxes have separate incidents; the stall explanation only attributes a provider or sandbox that can explain this squad's pending demand.

## Foreground progress and the demand clock

Each runnable stream attempt has its own last-service/readmission clock, separate from the original backlog creation time. Its clock is the latest of:

- Stream/flow creation and the attempt's durable dispatch receipt: either its exact assignment inbox row or its inline response-assignment transition.
- Closed waits that blocked this attempt or the whole stream, and the owner's ended slot subscription when admission makes its work eligible again.
- Its latest attributed execution's claimed `run_started_at`, persisted assistant steps, and successful completion (`ended_at`). Progress survives execution completion; going idle between turns does not turn seconds-old work into an hours-old stall.

Pinned execution `flow_context` must match both the stream and, for mapped running attempts, the attempt. An execution without pinned provenance can supply a legacy fallback only when its owner has no other active/queued stream association. Assistant steps must belong to the exact agent/execution, not predate the claimed start, and not be in the future. The existing agent/execution message index supports these lookups. Worker/lease heartbeats, agent updates, human traffic, other attempts' executions and unrelated squad activity do not advance the clock.

Participants who are alternatives for one legacy/delivery route or unpinned attempt share its newest applicable progress clock. Historical attempt bindings alone do not add routing participants. Across independent runnable attempts, the oldest group clock wins: a progressing or supervising sibling cannot reset a stale sibling's deadline. A running/stopping execution with progress in the last 30 minutes is still active work rather than idle backlog. Once that evidence expires, its otherwise runnable attempt counts as demand again, anchored to its actual last progress rather than stream creation. A completed turn's progress receives the same bounded 30-minute window; it is not discarded at completion. Fresh dispatch and slot promotion also get a new eligibility window instead of inheriting the stream's entire waiting age. A requeued execution similarly retains its own claimed-start/assistant-step clock rather than reverting to its original queue creation time; another execution cannot advance that clock.

A single foreground operation with no persisted assistant step for 30 minutes does not renew foreground evidence. Use a supervised event monitor for long quiet background waiting, not repeated status output.

## Capacity and supervised waiting

A queued slot subscription exempts its attributed current, pinned stream attempt for as long as it stays queued. Waiting for capacity is a known reason for an idle attempt, not a stall, however long the queue is: on a busy machine host the budget can be held by other squads for longer than the pool's claim timeout. (An earlier version bounded the exemption by the claim timeout, so a 45-minute `heavy-work` queue on a full host produced an "unexplained stall" alert telling people to check sandboxes and worker pickup.) Attribution requires an execution of that exact stream/attempt and participant spanning the enqueue time, matching the work-stream slot-wait projection (`loadStreamSlotWaits`). Leftover subscriptions from earlier attempts, other streams, legacy/unpinned ownership, disabled/unregistered pools, terminal waiters and foreign-squad owners cannot suppress that demand. When the subscription is granted or canceled, the attempt is ordinary demand again with a fresh readmission clock, so an agent that does not resume after its grant is still flagged 30 minutes later.

A slot subscription does not prevent independent execution pickup or inbox/schedule delivery, so none of those demand sources is suppressed merely because its owner is queued for capacity. This applies equally to squad-only pools, capped host-shared pools and host-shared budget queues. The alert code never recomputes capacity, clears subscriptions, requeues work or changes a pool. Ended valid subscriptions retain the existing bounded readmission clock.

[Live monitors](agent-background-monitors.md#quiet-monitors-and-stall-alerts) keep their existing 150-second process-confirmation window. Managed local apps keep their existing two-hour start/owner-check-in window. Both remain agent-attributed, not squad-wide activity exemptions.

### Long capacity waits

An abnormally long queue is its own incident, `slot_capacity_wait`, one episode per pool (`apps/core/src/services/fleet-alerts/slot-capacity.ts`). Each minute the fleet reconciler reads every enabled pool of a live squad that has queued waiters (dormant, terminated and foreign owners excluded). A pool is overdue when its oldest waiter has been queued longer than the pool's claim timeout plus 15 minutes (`SLOT_CAPACITY_WAIT_MARGIN_MS`): within the timeout, every holder that does not renew has released or expired, so a healthy queue has moved. The incident resolves, with a recovery notice to whoever was alerted, once no waiter is that old, normally because the oldest waiters were granted. Detection never claims, cancels, reorders or renews anything.

The alert names the pool, the queue length, the oldest wait and the host budget, and states that the queued work is waiting for capacity, not stalled. Its cause and suggestion follow from counts alone:

| Cause code                 | When                                                                                  | Suggested action                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `slot-host-held-elsewhere` | Host-shared pool, the machine host's budget is full, and other squads hold some of it | Nothing needs restarting; queued agents get a unit when a holder releases or expires. If it recurs, raise the host budget in Settings → System or ask an operator to review heavy work on the host. |
| `slot-held-by-squad`       | The pool, or the host budget, is full of this squad's own claims                      | Check holders with `ficus slot list`; a holder that has finished should release its claim.                                                                                                          |
| `slot-queue-not-granted`   | Capacity is free but the queue has not been granted                                   | Check the pool, then ask an authorized operator to check slot promotion.                                                                                                                            |

Other squads appear only as the number of host units they hold, never by name, agent, stream or pool. The audience is manager first, with humans after the standard 15-minute fallback if it persists (immediately when the squad has no valid manager). The manager's copy tells it not to restart queued agents or cancel their subscriptions.

## Human questions and independent work

An open human question excludes its waiting stream/attempt and the exact workflow assignment inbox messages whose acceptance is fenced by that wait. The fleet regression tests create a real blocking question and reconcile the full demand snapshot into incidents: an old stream waiting only for its human input stays quiet, with no outage or slot evidence needed. Both attempt-scoped and whole-stream waits retain this behavior. When the wait closes, an old undelivered workflow assignment gets a fresh eligibility clock from the applicable closed wait instead of inheriting its old inbox creation age.

A question is not a blanket agent admission stop. `Agent.queueExecution` fences assignment messages through `lockFlowInboxDelivery`, but independently accepted control turns can still be queued and picked up. Their `flow_context` is captured for attribution/usage even when the owning attempt is waiting; that field alone does not prove the queued work is blocked. Existing queued turns and due startup retries remain executable under pickup's normal eligibility rules. The tests verify both real acceptance and the pickup claim while replacing only the external model/box runner.

Similarly, a scheduled inbox action carries schedule provenance, not an attempt assignment, and can actually emit independent reminder/control work while a question is open. Ordinary independent mail and current wait-resolution controls remain deliverable. Human answers use their dedicated durable answer-delivery protocol, not the ordinary inbox batch; a saved answer can be delivered while a different question on the same attempt still waits. Such independently actionable work remains demand and cannot borrow a question's closure clock to conceal an existing stall.

These distinctions do not authorize continuing the blocked task or clearing its question. A report that a stream is waiting does not, by itself, establish whether another queued turn, inbox item or due schedule is also waiting. Classify each source by its actual acceptance/delivery provenance rather than suppressing the entire agent or inferring an outage.

## Answer delivery lifecycle

A saved answer's inbox row is executable demand only while both of these hold:

1. The answer-delivery protocol still owes delivery: the question is `answered` and its delivery status is `pending` or `delivering`. Ordinary inbox delivery never picks an answer row up, so a row whose question failed delivery terminally (a superseded attempt or exhausted retries), or that has no question record, cannot be served by any worker. A terminal failure is surfaced by the question's own delivery-failure alert and stays in the Action Center, where a retry is offered when one can work. Its inbox row is kept unread in history; the stall detector never marks it read.
2. The question still belongs to the agent (`isCurrentQuestionAttempt`): the attempt it was asked from is running, or it is the attempt the delivery participant delivers from while the stream is still active or queued. Finishing or canceling the stream ends that, even though the flow run keeps its `completion-ready` state. A rework or new flow version ends it too. Delivery then settles the answer as superseded without waking the agent, and records why under the stream lock: `lockFlowInboxDelivery` throws `FlowWaitStreamEndedError` (a `FlowWaitSupersededError`) when the stream's status is `done` or `canceled`, and plain `FlowWaitSupersededError` when the stream is still live and only the attempt was replaced. The saved delivery error is the cause, so it is never inferred later from a stream status that may have changed since.

   Once a stream has finished or been canceled, nobody is woken for a late answer: the failure alert and the Action Center card are the only notice. The alert says so plainly: the answer "went nowhere: the work stream had already finished when the answer arrived, so it was not delivered to the agent", with "had already been canceled" for a canceled stream. A replaced attempt on a live stream keeps its wording: "the workflow attempt the question was asked from was replaced before the answer arrived". Both add that retrying will not deliver it and that the answer can be sent to the agent as a message if it still applies. The Action Center card carries the same cause as `answerDelivery.refusal` (`stream-done`, `stream-canceled` or `attempt-replaced`), says the answer was not delivered instead of "confirmation was not recorded", and offers no Retry (`canRetry` is false). Answers refused before this cause was recorded keep the replaced-attempt wording.

Within those rules an idle agent's undelivered answer alerts after the normal 30-minute window, measured from the row's creation. A dormant agent's answer counts the same way, because an answer is wake-eligible mail and delivery wakes the agent. An agent with pending dormancy, a terminated agent or an agent on a paused stream does not count. The fixtures in `apps/core/src/services/fleet-alerts/answer-demand.test.ts` cover the reported shape (a merge answer that failed as superseded, after which the stream finished and the agent stayed idle), the race where the stream finishes, is canceled or has its attempt replaced between persisting and sending the answer (each recording its own cause, alert text and Action Center refusal), a genuinely stuck answer that must still alert, and the dormant cases.

## Alert and recovery

The durable incident store retains its 30-minute demand window, deduplicated manager-first delivery, delayed human fallback and recovery cooldown. Original backlog age remains visible separately from the last-service clock that determines the deadline. Clearing all executable demand, or observing fresh scoped service/readmission after an incident began, resolves it; pending alert deliveries are canceled and only audiences that received an alert receive recovery. If a sweep missed the entire fresh-progress interval, the incident's clock advances to the actual evidence without inventing a healthy recovery, and already-expired demand can still alert. A healthy unrelated worker or a worker restart cannot resolve a scoped stall. Aggregate-only reconciliation callers without a scoped clock retain the older run-start/cold-start fallback.

The generic explanation says pending work has no fresh execution progress, rather than claiming that no agent run started. Its suggested read-only commands are `ficus squad sandbox-status <squad-id>` and `ficus agent sandbox-status <agent-id>`. The host-only `worker` group is not offered to squad agents; worker-pickup inspection is routed to an authorized operator. The removed `ficus machines list` command is not present in current fleet-alert source. Command-reference tests check the real CLI tree, applicable default-role permissions and host-filtered squad-agent command index; generic stall guidance is checked for default workers as well as managers. Machine/host diagnostics still require the operator's normal authorization; an alert grants no operational recovery authority.
